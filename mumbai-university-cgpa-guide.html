How to Calculate Mumbai University CGPA to Percentage (2026 Guide)

Updated October 2026 • About 6 minute read

Important: This guide is for general information only. The percentage on your official marksheet or university certificate is the only figure that counts. Always verify with your official marksheet.

If you study at an institution affiliated to the University of Mumbai (MU), your results arrive as grades and grade points, not as a plain percentage. Yet job applications, scholarship forms and many admission portals still ask for "percentage". This guide explains how MU's grading system works, how the classic 7.1 × CGPA + 11 formula is used, why people are confused about the 2026 rules, and how to get an official percentage certificate.

What is a CGPA? Understanding the basics

CGPA stands for Cumulative Grade Point Average. It is a single number, usually on a 10-point scale, that summarises your performance across all semesters of a programme. It is built from smaller pieces, so it helps to understand each one.

Credits

Every course carries a number of credits, which reflect its weight in the curriculum. A four-credit course influences your result more than a one-credit practical. Credits are fixed by the programme structure and are printed on your marksheet.

Grade points

In each course you earn a letter grade, and each letter grade maps to a grade point (for example, a higher grade gives a higher point value). The mapping between marks, letter grades and grade points is defined in the university's ordinance for your scheme.

SGPA (Semester Grade Point Average)

SGPA measures a single semester. It is the credit-weighted average of the grade points you earned that semester:

  • SGPA = Σ (credits × grade points) ÷ Σ credits

Example: suppose you take three courses with 4, 3 and 3 credits and earn grade points of 8, 9 and 7. Your total weighted points are (4×8) + (3×9) + (3×7) = 32 + 27 + 21 = 80, and the total credits are 10. Your SGPA is 80 ÷ 10 = 8.00.

CGPA (Cumulative Grade Point Average)

CGPA combines all your semesters. The usual approach is to weight each semester's SGPA by that semester's total credits:

  • CGPA = Σ (semester credits × semester SGPA) ÷ Σ semester credits

Example: Semester 1 has 20 credits with an SGPA of 7.50, and Semester 2 has 22 credits with an SGPA of 8.00. The CGPA is (20×7.50 + 22×8.00) ÷ 42 = (150 + 176) ÷ 42 = 7.76. Your marksheet usually shows the CGPA already, so you rarely need to compute it yourself, but knowing the method helps you spot errors.

The classic formula: 7.1 × CGPA + 11

The conversion most often associated with Mumbai University engineering results is:

  • Percentage = 7.1 × CGPA + 11

Because it is linear, you simply multiply your CGPA by 7.1 and add 11. Here are a few worked examples:

Notice that the formula is deliberately not "CGPA × 10". An 8.0 CGPA converts to 67.8%, not 80%. Many students are surprised by this gap, and it is one reason you should always check which conversion a recruiter or university expects before filling in a form.

The 2026 confusion: what is actually going on?

If you search for "Mumbai University CGPA to percentage 2026", you will find conflicting answers. Some websites repeat 7.1 × CGPA + 11. Others quote a different multiplier or apply a generic rule such as CGPA × 9.5 or CGPA × 10 to every university. Some pages claim a "new 2026 formula" without linking to any circular.

At the time of writing (October 2026), we could not find an official, published revision of the Mumbai University formula that we could verify. That does not mean a change can never happen. Universities do revise grading rules, and different programmes (engineering, science, commerce, arts, and the 7-point grading schemes) can follow different conversion rules. A figure that is correct for one scheme may be wrong for another.

The practical lesson is simple:

  • Treat third-party calculators, including ours, as a quick estimate.
  • Look for a conversion statement printed on your marksheet or grade card.
  • Check the latest circulars on the official University of Mumbai website or ask your college's examination section.
  • If an employer or university needs a precise figure, ask for an official certificate (see below).

How to get an official percentage certificate

When a form demands an authoritative percentage, an estimate will not do. You need a certificate issued by your institution that states the percentage equivalent of your CGPA and the formula used. The usual process looks like this:

  1. Contact the right office. For most students this is the examination section of their college. Some colleges refer you to the university's examination department.
  2. Write an application. State your name, seat number or PRN, programme, and the purpose of the certificate (for example, "for higher studies" or "for employment").
  3. Attach documents. Typically you submit copies of your marksheets or grade cards, a photo ID, and sometimes your college ID. Carry originals for verification.
  4. Pay the fee, if any. Fees differ between institutions, so confirm the amount before applying.
  5. Collect the certificate. One Mumbai-affiliated college states that processing takes roughly 4 to 5 working days, but timelines vary and can be longer at busy periods.

Apply well before your deadline, and request more than one original if you are applying to several places. For a more detailed walkthrough, read our separate guide on getting a CGPA to percentage certificate from your university.

Tips for converting your CGPA correctly

  • Use your final CGPA from the consolidated marksheet, not a single semester's SGPA.
  • Keep two decimal places while calculating and round only at the end.
  • Never type a self-calculated percentage into a government or university form unless the instructions allow it.
  • Save a PDF of your marksheet and any circular that mentions the formula.

Frequently asked questions

What is the Mumbai University CGPA to percentage formula?

The widely published formula is Percentage = 7.1 × CGPA + 11. It is commonly quoted for engineering programmes, but schemes differ, so check the rule printed on your marksheet or in the latest university circular.

Did Mumbai University change its CGPA to percentage formula in 2026?

We could not find an official revised 2026 formula at the time of writing. Several websites quote different figures, which causes confusion. Rely on your official marksheet, college exam section or a university circular rather than third-party sites.

How do I get an official percentage certificate from Mumbai University?

Apply in writing to your college's examination section or the university's examination department, attach your marksheets and ID proof, and pay any prescribed fee. Processing times vary; one affiliated college states about 4 to 5 working days.
