SSC CGL Age Limit 2026: Eligibility & Relaxation Rules

Updated October 2026 • About 7 minute read

Note: Figures below are compiled from the SSC CGL 2026 notification as reported by coaching and news websites. Rules can differ by post and cycle, so always read the official notification on ssc.gov.in before applying.

Age is one of the first things the Staff Selection Commission (SSC) checks in a Combined Graduate Level (CGL) application. A single day can decide whether you are eligible, which is why aspirants search for the age limit again and again. This guide explains how SSC calculates age, why the cut-off date matters more than today's date, the age limits for different posts, the category-wise relaxations, and how to use an age calculator without mistakes.

How SSC calculates your age

SSC does not look at how old you are on the day you fill the form or sit the exam. Instead, it counts your age on a fixed cut-off date given in the notification. Your date of birth is taken from your matriculation or equivalent certificate, and your age is the number of completed years between that date and the cut-off.

The upper limit is normally written as "not more than" a number of years. In practice this means that you must not have completed the next year of age on the cut-off date. For example, a limit of 27 years means you can be 27 years and several months old, but you must not have turned 28 by the cut-off date.

The cut-off date concept: why it matters

For SSC CGL 2026, the notification was released on 21 May 2026, applications ran until 22 June 2026, and the cut-off date for age and educational qualification was reported as 1 August 2026. That means:

  • If you were 27 on the day you applied but turned 28 before 1 August 2026, you are over the limit for a 27-year post.
  • If you were 17 when you applied but turned 18 on or before 1 August 2026, you meet the minimum age.
  • Your age today, in October 2026, is irrelevant for this cycle.

Category-wise age relaxation

Reserved categories and some other groups get extra years on the upper age limit. The commonly reported relaxations are:

CategoryRelaxation
OBC3 years
SC / ST5 years
PwBD (General / Unreserved)10 years
PwBD (OBC)13 years

Ex-servicemen3 years, after deducting the military service rendered

The notification also lists other relaxations for specific groups, such as certain government employees and other special categories. Those are published in the official notice, so check it if you think you may qualify.

A worked example

Suppose you are an OBC candidate applying for a post with an 18–30 range. Your upper limit becomes 30 + 3 = 33 years as on 1 August 2026. A General candidate for the same post must be born on or after 2 August 1995, but an OBC candidate can be born on or after 2 August 1992. An SC candidate gets 35 years, which moves the earliest eligible birth date to 2 August 1990.

Points to remember about relaxation

  • Relaxation applies to the upper age limit, not the lower limit.
  • You must hold a valid certificate for your category, issued by the correct authority and in the prescribed format.
  • Disability relaxation requires a valid disability certificate meeting the benchmark criteria.
  • Claiming relaxation you are not entitled to can lead to rejection of your candidature, even after you have cleared stages.

How to use an age calculator correctly

An age calculator saves time, but only if you use it carefully. Follow these tips:

  1. Use the cut-off date, not today's date. Enter 1 August 2026 (or whatever the current notification says) as the "age as on" date.
  2. Enter the date of birth exactly as printed on your certificate. If your school record and Aadhaar differ, SSC goes by the matriculation certificate.
  3. Read years, months and days. Being "27 years, 11 months, 30 days" is still within a 27-year limit; "28 years, 0 months, 0 days" is not.
  4. Add relaxation separately. Calculate your age first, then compare it with the upper limit plus your category relaxation.
  5. Double-check edge cases. If your birthday falls within a day or two of the cut-off, re-verify with the official notice and your documents.
  6. Re-check for every exam. The cut-off date changes from one notification to the next, so last year's result will not apply.

Frequently asked questions

What is the cut-off date for SSC CGL 2026 age calculation?

For SSC CGL 2026, age and educational qualification are assessed as on 1 August 2026, as reported from the official notification. Your age on today's date or on the day you apply does not matter.

What is the age relaxation for OBC, SC and ST candidates in SSC CGL?

The reported relaxation is 3 years for OBC and 5 years for SC/ST candidates on the upper age limit. Persons with benchmark disabilities get 10 years (General), 13 years (OBC) or 15 years (SC/ST). Always confirm in the official notification.

Can I use an online age calculator to check SSC CGL eligibility?

Yes, as long as you enter the cut-off date from the official notification, not today's date. Use the result to check whether your date of birth falls inside the permitted range, and keep any category relaxation in mind.
